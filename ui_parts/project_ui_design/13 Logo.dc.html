13 · Logo

A mark for something that holds every credential you own

Three directions. All three are built from geometry and type rather than illustration, all three survive at 16 px in a system tray, and all three work in one colour — those are the conditions for looking trustworthy rather than merely decorated.

?
I cannot generate images. These are vector marks I constructed from precise coordinates and a typeface. Direction B's letterform is set in IBM Plex Sans rather than drawn — in production it would need a type designer to cut it properly.
01

Three directions

ARouterecommended

A solid seal with one channel routed through it, climbing two steps as it crosses. The seal is the vault; the channel is the thing Remoter actually does that its competitors do badly — reaching a machine through another one. No metaphor borrowed from banking, no padlock.

BMonogramalready in the mockups
R

The safe answer, and the one every title bar in screens 01–11 already draws. Monograms read as institutional, which is most of what "trustworthy" means visually. It says nothing about the product, and there are a great many teal R squares in the world.

CHop

Two endpoints and a bastion between them, drawn as a diamond so the intermediate reads differently from the ends. The most explicit about jump hosts and the lightest on the page — but it has no container, so it needs its own padding everywhere and makes a weak app icon.

02

One step or two — your call

I drew A with a single riser first. Looking at it at size, a single edge-to-edge cut splits the square into two interlocking halves, which is the exact geometry of an S — the wrong letter for Remoter. Two risers read as a staircase instead. The rest of this sheet uses two.

A1One step
Calmer and heavier. Holds up better below 16 px. Reads as a letter that is not ours.
A2Two stepsin use below
Reads as a route climbing through two hops, which is the product. Busier at 16 px, and the corner radius had to come down to 10 to fit the exits.
03

Direction A, worked through

Construction
Everything sits on a 48 unit grid. The corner radius is 10, so it stays a square that has been softened rather than a lozenge.
The channel is 6 deep and crosses on two 45° risers of 7 units each, entering at y 27 and leaving at y 13. Two steps, not one: a single riser splits the square into an accidental letterform, and two read as what they are — a route climbing through hops. It runs edge to edge, because the route passes through rather than stopping inside.

Do not
Never rotate. The channel climbs left to right; that is the meaning.
No gradients. It must print, emboss and rasterise at 16 px.
Never green, amber or red. Those four hues mean session state.
Never stretch. The risers are 45° and stay 45°.

05

Where I would push back on myself

1
Rounded-square marks are everywhere in developer tooling. A earns its place through the cut, not the container — and a cut is a subtle thing to hang an identity on. Worth testing against a real competitor set before committing.
2
The wordmark is a typeface used unmodified. That is a legitimate starting point and a poor finishing point — the R and t in particular want custom drawing.
3
I have not checked any of these against existing trademarks. That is a search someone has to run before a GPL project puts a mark on a release page.
